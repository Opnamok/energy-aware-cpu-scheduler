process list

pid label arrival burst (work) priority
burst is work, not time. It is the work a process needs at the reference clock (3.0 GHz). At 2.0 GHz the same work takes 1.5× as long; at 1.2 GHz, 2.5×. That is why lowering the clock saves power but can cost completion time — and why the Gantt chart shows half-unit boundaries once a slower state is used.

repeated trials

One run proves nothing: random workloads vary by far more than the differences between policies. Each trial here generates a fresh workload from the seed and gives every policy the identical workload, so the paired differences cancel the workload noise. The same seed always reproduces the same numbers.

parameter sweep

Sweeping one parameter at a time shows how sensitive each policy is to it. p_static is the most instructive axis: as the leakage floor rises, the energy-optimal clock moves from LOW to MED to HIGH — which is why real processors do not simply run as slowly as possible.
